Opening Clipnest the first time

Clipnest isn't signed with a paid Microsoft or Apple certificate yet, so your computer asks once whether you trust it. After that it opens normally.

Windows

  1. Open the downloaded Clipnest-Setup file.
  2. If you see “Windows protected your PC”, click More info.
  3. Click Run anyway and follow the setup.

Mac

  1. Open the .dmg and drag Clipnest into Applications.
  2. Open Clipnest. When macOS warns you, click Done (not Move to Trash).
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

Says “Clipnest is damaged”? Open Terminal, run this once, then open Clipnest again:

xattr -cr /Applications/Clipnest.app

Questions

Is Clipnest really free?

Yes. No ads, no trial, no premium version. It's open source under the MIT license, so anyone can read the code on GitHub.

Is it safe?

All the code is public on GitHub, and every download is built automatically by GitHub from that code. The warning when you first open it only means the app isn't signed with a paid certificate yet.

Why does it ask me to log in for some links?

Instagram, Vimeo, private Facebook or X posts, and sometimes YouTube only show videos to logged-in people. In Clipnest open Settings → Logins and choose the browser where you're logged in. Firefox works most reliably on Windows.

Can it download from Spotify?

No. Spotify protects its music with DRM (copy protection), and Clipnest doesn't break copy protection.

Where are my files saved?

In your Downloads folder. You can pick another folder in Settings. Clipnest never replaces a file you already have; a second copy is saved as “Name (2)”.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes, with Clipnest Web. Turn it on in Clipnest's Settings on your computer and scan the QR code with your phone. You paste links in your phone's browser, the download runs on your computer, and you save the file to your phone. Clipnest must be open on the computer, and both need to be on the same Wi-Fi.

What am I allowed to download?

Clipnest is a tool. Download only content you own, that's licensed for reuse (for example Creative Commons), or that you otherwise have the right to save. Respect creators' copyright and each site's terms.
